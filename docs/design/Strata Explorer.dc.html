Interaction note — not part of the UI drill-down
The tile you clicked becomes the frame. On click, the target tile animates from its position in the parent map to fill the plot over 260 ms, cubic-out, while its children cross-fade in at 40% opacity rising to full. Siblings do not fly out; they fade to nothing in place. The effect is a camera push, not a card shuffle, so you keep your bearings.
Colour is inherited, not reassigned. A child keeps the hue of its top-level ancestor and steps one lightness level per depth. Users is --s1 at every depth, so a folder you know stays the colour you learnt on the Timeline.
Three children and two thousand are the same code path. Below 40 children every tile is labelled. Above that, tiles smaller than 400 px² merge into one +n smaller tile drawn in --other, which is itself clickable and expands into the sibling list. The list, not the map, is the exhaustive view — it is sorted, searchable and virtualised, and it is where a 2,041-child directory is actually read.
You are here is stated three ways. The breadcrumb marks the current segment in --fg-1 on --bg-3; the header repeats the full path with its size, file count and share of parent; and the map's own frame carries a 1px amber edge. Backspace goes up, and the browser Back button does too — every drill-down is a history entry.
