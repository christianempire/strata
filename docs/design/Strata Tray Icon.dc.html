Light-taskbar approach · contained ground
The icon brings its own background. A near-black #0f0d0b ground fills 14 of the 16 pixels, so the bands are always amber-on-near-black regardless of what is behind them. Contrast is a property of the artwork, not of the taskbar. The alternative — a single mid-tone with enough chroma to survive both ends — was drawn as direction C and rejected: amber holds 10.1:1 on a dark taskbar but 1.7:1 on a light one, which fails the 3:1 minimum for non-text contrast, and it rules out near-white as a state colour.
The rim is what earns the ground. A dark block alone would dissolve into a dark taskbar, so the outer 1px ring is drawn in the state colour. On light backgrounds the rim adds nothing — for the scanning state it is invisible against white — but the dark ground provides the silhouette, so the mark simply reads 1px smaller. Neither background ever loses both the rim and the ground.
One artwork, no variants. Windows does not swap tray icons on theme change and this design does not need it to. The third strip above is #1f1f1f, the actual default Windows taskbar, because pure black flatters a dark-ground icon in a way the real thing does not.
Which element carries state
Colour of the whole mark — rim and bands together. One value changes and every pixel of ink follows it, so the state is legible from the corner of the eye at 16px without the shape having to be re-read. Amber idle, near-white scanning, warm orange deferred, grey paused.
Deferred also changes form, because colour is not enough. #ff9868 and #f8ac3d are 40° apart in hue at nearly the same lightness — side by side they separate, but idle-versus-deferred is the most consequential distinction the icon makes and it has to survive being seen alone. So the bands collapse from 2/1/3 px to three 1px rules: solid block versus sparse block, obvious at a glance and still obvious in greyscale. The semantics come out right as well — the layers are outlined where they would be, but nothing is being deposited.
Cyan was rejected deliberately. --neg means shrinkage in every chart in the app; spending it on deferral would break that. Red was never a candidate — deferring is the software working correctly.

Five rects, no strokes, no gradients, no curves, nothing thinner than 1px. Band widths 10 / 6 / 10 and thicknesses 2 / 1 / 3 give an uneven rhythm, so the stack reads as a section rather than a barcode. The 1px rim is a rect pair rather than a stroke, so it cannot land on a half-pixel.
PNGs at 20 and 24 are not integer multiples of 16, so every rect edge is rounded to the nearest device pixel at export rather than resampled — the bands stay crisp and no band ever rounds away to nothing. The 1px rim becomes 1px at 16 and 20, 2px at 32, and scales with the grid above that, which keeps its weight consistent at 200% DPI.
